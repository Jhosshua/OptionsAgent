OptionsAgent

A paper-trading bot that sells credit spreads. Claude proposes. Code decides.
PAPER ONLY
CREDIT SPREADS
0DTE SCALP ARMED
LLM NEVER PICKS STRIKES
OptionsAgent trades one strategy on a $100,000 Alpaca paper account: defined-risk credit spreads on 13 liquid names under $50. Once a day it asks Claude for trade ideas, but Claude only gets to say which ticker, which direction, and how confident. Every strike, expiration, size, and exit is chosen by fixed Python rules called rails. If an idea fails any rail, it dies. Most days, nothing trades, and that is by design. A second engine, fully isolated from the first, scalps 0DTE SPY and QQQ opening-range breakouts intraday for a fixed $250 a shot.
STEP 1
Cron wakes it
Weekdays 10:15 to 10:27 ET, once a day. A lock file blocks double runs. If the broker clock check fails or the market is shut, it skips the day.
STEP 2
Snapshot
Archives the full option chain (quotes, IV, greeks) for all 13 watchlist names, then builds a market context bundle per name.
STEP 3
Claude proposes
The local Claude CLI (sonnet, no tools, no web) reads the bundle and proposes ticker, direction, conviction 0 to 1, thesis. Strikes and size are banned from its output. CLI failure means zero trades.
STEP 4
Rails veto or size
Deterministic gate: conviction floor 0.60, max 6 open positions, phase allowlist. Size scales from 30% to 100% of available options buying power.
STEP 5
Code picks the contract
Short leg 15 to 30 delta, width capped at $2, 30 to 45 DTE. Bullish sells a put spread, bearish sells a call spread. One combo limit order at the net credit.
STEP 6
The winner profile
The spread must also match one of three replayed winners: CCL bullish put, SOFI bullish put, F bearish call. Everything else is vetoed. Quiet days are normal.
MEANWHILE, EVERY 20 MIN
A separate exit sweep runs with no LLM at all: take profit at 50% of the credit, stop out at 2x credit (only after 10:00 ET and only after two consecutive sweeps agree, so one wide quote cannot force a liquidation), force close at 21 DTE, and check ex-dividend dates before any short call sits through one.
EVERY MINUTE, 09:33 TO 15:50 ET
The 0DTE scalper (no LLM): mark the 09:30 to 09:33 opening range in SPY and QQQ, wait for a 1-minute close outside it with 1.5x average volume, confirm on the next bar plus VWAP, then buy an ATM call (break up) or put (break down) for $250, max 2 trades a day, one at a time, no entries after 11:30 ET. Exits: +50% target, -30% stop (-60% while the breakout holds), 15-minute theta cut, and a mandatory 15:50 ET flatten so nothing rides into 0DTE auto-exercise.

FAQ
Everything below is verified from the current code and the live paper account, not from the older docs.
What strategy is running right now, exactly?
Vertical credit spreads only. Bullish thesis sells a put spread, bearish sells a call spread, never neutral. Short leg between 15 and 30 delta, long leg further out on the same expiry, width capped at $2.00, entry between 30 and 45 DTE. The spread is submitted as a single MLEG combo limit order priced at the net credit, order IDs prefixed oa-.
How does position sizing work?
Position budget = conviction-scaled share of available options buying power. At the 0.60 floor you get 30% of what is free, scaling linearly to 100% at 0.85 and above. Contracts = budget ÷ (width × 100). If the budget buys less than one contract, the trade is skipped. Each fill inside a cycle shrinks the base the next proposal is sized against.
100% of buying power on one idea sounds aggressive.
It is deliberate operator policy: "use all the cash, not necessarily all at once." The practical brakes are the $2 width cap (max loss ≈ $200 per spread, 0.2% of the account), the 6 position cap, the broker's own buying power rejection, and an optional tighten-only kill switch (OA_MAX_POSITION_USD) that can clamp any single position.
Where does market data come from?
Option chains and quotes from a read-only Public.com sidecar; execution and account state at Alpaca paper; equity snapshots from the broker. Every entry cycle also archives the full chain (quotes, IV, greeks) to disk so a backtester can be built later from real captured data.
What is the winner profile gate and why does it veto so much?
A replay of every archived fill from July 8 to July 31 found three profiles that actually made money: CCL bullish puts (credit ≥ $0.29, width ≥ $1.50), SOFI bullish puts (credit ≥ $0.23, width ≥ $1.00), and F bearish calls (credit ≥ $0.06, width ≤ $0.50). Those three are hard-coded. Anything else fails closed. It is openly an in-sample overfit, held frozen on purpose so the account collects clean out-of-sample evidence on whether the profile means anything.
Walk me through the exits.
A no-LLM sweep every 20 minutes during market hours. Priority: dividend risk first (a short call sitting through an ex-date where the dividend exceeds the same-strike put gets closed), then the stop, then DTE, then profit. Profit target is 50% of the credit received. The stop is 2x credit but only counts from 10:00 ET and needs two consecutive sweeps to confirm, so a single wide opening quote cannot trigger a forced liquidation. Everything force-closes at 21 DTE. There is no end-of-day flatten for spreads because nothing here is 0DTE.
What about assignment and overnight gap risk?
Verticals are defined risk: worst case is width minus credit, no more, regardless of assignment mechanics. The 2x credit stop is a loss control, not a guarantee. A gap through the short strike overnight still costs the full width, roughly $200 per spread at the cap. Six concurrent positions put the theoretical worst day near $1,200, just over 1% of the $100,000 account.
Is there a daily loss limit for the seller?
No dedicated one. The brakes are structural: $2 max width, 6 positions, one short entry window per day (10:15 to 10:27 ET), and the winner profile that rejects most ideas before they exist. If that answer makes you uncomfortable, that is a fair reaction; it is a paper account built to learn the distribution.
What does the LLM see, and can it be manipulated by the data?
It sees a JSON bundle: the phase, the allowed strategies, and per-ticker market context (price levels, volume, recent movement). The system prompt states the context is data, never instructions. It runs with tools disabled, no session persistence, no web access, and its output is validated against a strict schema before anything downstream sees it.
What does the 0DTE scalper actually do?
A second, fully isolated engine (own files, own oas- order prefix, no LLM anywhere in it) trades SPY and QQQ same-day options. It marks the first 3 minutes' high and low as the opening range, then buys an ATM call or put only when a 1-minute bar closes outside the range on 1.5x average volume AND the next bar confirms with price still on the right side of VWAP. $250 per trade, max 2 a day, one at a time, each direction once per symbol, no entries after 11:30 ET (12 of 12 late entries in its own history lost), +50% target, -30% stop that widens to -60% while the breakout holds, 15-minute theta cut, mandatory flatten by 15:50 ET.
How do I know what it did on any given day?
Three places. The dashboard on the following pages (live at 127.0.0.1:8765, read-only, it cannot submit orders). The decision log at data/decisions.jsonl, which records every proposal, every veto reason, and every order. And data/structures.jsonl, the append-only record of what opened and closed. Today's log shows 8 proposals and 8 vetoes with reasons.
What stops a bug from doing something catastrophic?
Layers. Rails are hard-coded in Python so no config edit or model output can loosen them; env overrides may only tighten. The broker client refuses non-paper endpoints. Entry is one locked window a day. The exit sweep reconciles the registry against live broker positions and alerts on anything that vanished (assignment, expiry, manual close) instead of silently dropping it.
